Per-harness summary omp — $0.279. The most expensive and the most thorough. Every specific claim I checked verified: business_facts.sector_data.unique_metrics with proved_reserves_bbl_oil_eq/production_bpd, the tests/arche/conftest.py monkeypatch pattern, kaos.config, .card/.grid-2/muted/fetchJson. Concrete test fixture math is correct ($80 price, 1e9 bbl, 1e8 bbl/yr, costs 10+8+4+3 → breakeven $25, life 10y, intrinsic $55e9). Best snapshot/partial-failure semantics. Pays 16× pi’s price for the best plan. qwen — $0.139. Correct module layering (arche ingestion, nous extraction, kaos tables, kosmos reads), no factual errors, and the only plan that distinguishes total-reserve vs economically-viable exhaustion years — closest reading of the ambiguous original request. Sound deferral policy with reserved API fields. Lacks omp’s concrete anchor-level specificity. pi — $0.017. Astonishing value: cleanest module-responsibility split of all six, correct reserve-life labeling, snapshot versioning, good missing-data rules. Weaknesses: introduces proven-plus-probable/BOE normalization nobody asked for, and ends with 6 open questions (EIA endpoints, BOE constants, association definition, FX, partial-failure policy) — structurally excellent but less immediately executable. dsh — $0.126. Only harness to correctly explain why companies can’t reuse the existing companies table (CIK NOT NULL — verified). Strong strict-> viability semantics and the best test-list per dollar after pi. But: chose /oil while the user’s clarifications to 5 other harnesses say /energy, and its ingestion section defers everything to “future jobs” — v1 ships a page with no data path. cline — $0.073. Clear, disciplined plan: EIA-first, bounded watchlist, honest deferral of economics, “unavailable not zero” throughout. But its standalone energy/ package + JSON artifact contradicts the documented kaos storage invariant, and its verification is weakest — explicitly “do not add automated tests,” compileall only. opencode — $0.120. Same artifact-based architecture as cline (same kaos-invariant divergence — notable since kaos/storage.py:4-8 is explicit about it), same correct deferral semantics, decent verification checklist (import without network, empty states). Otherwise the most generic of the six — fewest verified specifics, no concrete fixtures, “will” statements instead of decisions. Odd opener: “This plan ignores kosmos/cline.md.” Bottom line: omp if cost doesn’t matter; pi for quality-per-dollar by an order of magnitude; the group consensus architecture (kaos namespaced tables + arche ingestion + /energy + deferred economics with null-safe formulas, as in omp/qwen/pi/qwen) is what I’d implement.
